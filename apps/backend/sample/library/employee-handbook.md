## Working hours

The working week is thirty-seven and a half hours, and when you work them is agreed with your team rather than set by the company. Most teams keep a shared core from ten until three, so that meetings have somewhere to go, and leave the rest to each person. Depot and support teams work in shifts, which their leads publish four weeks ahead.

Working from home is normal for roles that allow it. Tell your team where you are working each week in the shared calendar, and come to the office for the days your team has agreed to meet in person. Overtime is the exception rather than the plan: if you are regularly working more than your hours, raise it with your manager, because it usually means the work needs to change.

## Leave

Everybody has twenty-eight days of annual leave plus public holidays, and up to five days can be carried into the next year. Book leave in the people system at least two weeks ahead for anything longer than three days, so your team can plan around it. Your manager approves it; they can ask to move it, but they cannot cancel leave that was already approved.

If you are ill, tell your manager before the start of your working day and record it in the people system when you are back. Nobody needs a doctor's note for the first five days. Parental, compassionate and study leave are described in the policies linked from the people system, and People will talk any of them through with you.

## Expenses

Spend company money as if it were your own. Travel is booked through the travel portal, standard class for trains and economy for flights under six hours. Claim other expenses within a month, with a photograph of the receipt, and they are paid back with the next salary. Anything over five hundred euros needs your manager's approval before you spend it.
