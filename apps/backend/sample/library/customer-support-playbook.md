## Response times

An enterprise customer is answered within an hour, a mid-market customer within four, and everybody else within one working day. Answered means a person has read the ticket and told the customer what happens next; an automatic acknowledgement does not count, and neither does a question back that could have been answered from the account notes.

The clock runs in the customer's working hours, not ours. A ticket from Madrid at half past eight in the morning is due at half past nine Madrid time, whoever is on shift. The support queue shows each ticket's deadline in the responder's own time zone, coloured amber at half the window and red when it has passed.

## Escalation

A ticket the first responder cannot solve goes to the team that owns the system involved, with the customer told who has it now and when they will hear next. Nobody hands a ticket on without saying so, and nobody hands it on twice without involving the support lead.

An enterprise ticket that has been open for a full working day goes to the account manager as well, who decides whether to call the customer. A ticket that names a security concern, such as a suspicious sign-in, a leaked document or a message that looks like phishing, goes straight to the incident response rota, whatever the customer's tier.

## Closing a ticket

A ticket is closed when the customer agrees it is solved, or when they have not replied for five working days after we said it was. The closing note says what was wrong, what fixed it, and whether anything should change so it does not happen again. Those notes are what the quarterly account review reads.
