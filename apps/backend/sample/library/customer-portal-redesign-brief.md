## The problem

Customers use the portal for three things: to see where a delivery is, to download a proof of delivery, and to raise a support ticket. All three are possible today, and none of them is easy. Tracking a delivery takes four clicks from the home page, the proof of delivery is a scanned sheet that is often unreadable, and the ticket form asks for an internal reference most customers have never seen.

So customers phone instead. Last quarter the support team took more than two thousand calls asking where a delivery was, which is the single largest reason anybody called. Every one of those calls was a question the portal should have answered on its own, and each took a member of the team away from a ticket that needed a person.

![The redesign brief's illustration: an abstract blend of lavender and deep purple with soft circles of light](pictures/portal-sketch.png)

## What we will build

The first release puts the customer's open deliveries on the home page, each with its live status from the field tablets and, once delivered, the signature and photograph the driver recorded. The proof of delivery becomes a generated document rather than a scan.

The ticket form asks only for what the customer knows, such as the delivery, the site and what went wrong, and fills in the rest from the account. The release ships to the customers in the field tablet rollout's first wave, then to everyone else once the depot systems have caught up. Anything else customers have asked for, such as invoices, reports and user management, waits for a later release, so that the first one is small enough to ship this year.

## How we will know it worked

Calls asking where a delivery is should fall by half within a quarter of the release. The portal's own measure is the share of deliveries looked up without a call, which the support lead reports monthly. If calls do not fall, the project stops adding features and finds out why.
