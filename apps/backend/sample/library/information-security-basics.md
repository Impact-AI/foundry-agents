## Signing in

Every account here signs in with a passkey: the laptop's fingerprint reader or a security key, never a password typed into a page. If a page asks for a password anyway, stop and report it, because none of our own systems should. A passkey cannot be phished, which is the whole reason for using one.

Never approve a sign-in you did not start. If your phone asks you to confirm a sign-in while you are not signing in, decline it and tell the service desk; it means somebody else has tried. Shared accounts do not exist here. If a system seems to need one, ask the team that runs it for a named account instead, so that every action can be traced to the person who took it.

## Devices and data

Your laptop encrypts its disk and locks after five minutes. Lock it yourself whenever you step away, even in the office. Customer data stays in the systems built to hold it: not in a personal folder, not on a USB stick, not attached to an email to yourself so you can work on it at home.

Before you share a document, check who it is shared with. The document library marks who may read each document, and a link that anybody can open is never the right setting for customer data. When you leave a project, ask for your access to its systems to be removed; access that nobody needs is access nobody notices being used.

## Spotting a phishing message

A phishing message wants you to act before you think: a password that expires today, an invoice that must be paid now, a parcel that cannot be delivered. Check the sender's address, not the name shown above it, and hover over a link before you follow it. If anything feels wrong, forward the message to the security mailbox and delete it. Reporting a message that turns out to be genuine costs nobody anything.
