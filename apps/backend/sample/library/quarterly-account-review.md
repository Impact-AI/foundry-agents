## Before the meeting

Two weeks ahead, the account manager pulls the quarter together from the customer system: revenue against the plan, the support tickets opened and closed with their closing notes, the deliveries that went wrong, and anything the customer said they wanted last time. One page, no more. A review that needs a slide deck to explain the quarter is a review that is hiding something.

Send the page to the customer a week before the meeting and ask what they want to talk about. Then ask the teams who served them, whether delivery, support or a project, for anything the numbers do not show: a change of contact, a complaint made by phone, a site that is about to close. Agree with your manager what you will and will not offer before you are in the room.

## The meeting

Start with what the customer asked to discuss, not with our numbers. Then walk through the page together, the misses first. A customer who hears about a problem from us, with what we did about it, trusts the rest of the page more than one who has to find it.

Leave room for the customer's own news. A new warehouse, a merger or a change of buyer matters more to the next year than anything on the page, and it is often mentioned only in passing.

Close by agreeing at most three things that will be different next quarter, each with one owner and a date, and write them down in front of the customer. Three is a limit on purpose: a review that agrees ten actions usually finishes none of them.

## Afterwards

Within two working days, send the customer the agreed actions and record them in the customer system against the account. Each owner gets a reminder a week before their date. At the next review, those actions are the first thing on the page, done or not done.
